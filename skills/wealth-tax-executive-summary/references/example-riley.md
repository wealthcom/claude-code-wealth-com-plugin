## IRMAA positioning (Medicare premium surcharge)

IRMAA uses modified AGI (AGI plus tax-exempt interest), so the Rileys' MAGI is $800,238 ($791,038
+ $9,200). This 2025 return sets Medicare premiums for **2027** under the two-year lookback. The
2027 tiers are not published yet, so MAGI is measured against the most recent published year — the
**2026** married-filing-jointly tiers — as a proxy:

| 2026 MAGI (MFJ) | Part B / month | Part D add'l / month |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ $218,000 | $202.90 (standard) | — |
| $218,000–$274,000 | +$81.20 | +$14.50 |
| $274,000–$342,000 | +$202.90 | +$37.50 |
| $342,000–$410,000 | +$324.60 | +$60.40 |
| $410,000–$750,000 | +$446.30 | +$83.30 |
| ≥ $750,000 | +$487.00 | +$91.00 |

At $800,238 MAGI they sit in the top tier (≥ $750,000). Per enrolled spouse that's a Part B
premium of $689.90/month ($202.90 + $487.00) plus a $91.00/month Part D surcharge — $6,936/year
per person in surcharges above the standard premium. David is Medicare-eligible now; Victoria
reaches 65 in 2027. Household MAGI sets the tier for each enrolled spouse, so once both are
enrolled the surcharge doubles at the household level. The 2027 thresholds may shift when
they are published; measured against the 2026 tiers, this income lands in the top IRMAA tier.
